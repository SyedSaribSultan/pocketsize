"Nothing is uploaded", and how to check that yourself

Every image tool says it cares about your privacy. Almost all of them still send your picture to a server to do the work. Pocketsize does not: the compression runs inside your browser, on your own computer. Because that claim is worthless as words alone, this page shows you three ways to verify it, each in about a minute, none requiring any technical background beyond pressing a key.

Check 1

Watch the network

Open Pocketsize, then press F12 (or right-click the page and choose "Inspect") and click the Network tab. This panel shows every single thing a page sends or receives, and it cannot be fooled. Now drop a photo in and compress it. What you will see: the page downloading its own machinery (files ending in .js, .css, .wasm), a few small requests to e.syedsarib.com (our usage counter, explained below), and your photo appearing nowhere. No upload, no request carrying your file or its name. Click one of the counter's requests and you will see what it carries: that a page was visited, and totals such as how many pictures were done and how many bytes were saved. There is nothing else to send: the encoders themselves were downloaded to your machine, and your picture never leaves it.

Check 2

Read the rule that limits where the page can send anything

Not uploading is a choice; being unable to send things to a stranger is a guarantee, and Pocketsize ships with it. Every page is delivered with a security header called a Content Security Policy that your browser itself enforces. Ours says, among other things:

default-src 'none'; connect-src 'self' https://e.syedsarib.com; form-action 'none'

In plain words: this page may talk to its own address to fetch its own files, and to one other address, e.syedsarib.com, our usage counter, and to nowhere else, ever. If the code tried to send your picture to any other address (because of a bug, a compromise, or a change of heart), the browser would block the attempt. For the counter's one address, the guarantee is the code itself, which is open and short (js/analytics.js), and the Network panel, where every request it sends can be read. You can read the header itself in the same panel: click the first request, look under "Response Headers" for content-security-policy.

Check 3

Unplug the internet

The simplest proof of all. Visit Pocketsize once, then turn on airplane mode or pull the network cable, and keep compressing. The whole compressor, encoders included, keeps working with no connection at all, because after your first visit it lives on your device. A tool that uploaded your pictures could not do this.

What we do see

Honesty cuts both ways, so here is the whole picture. Like any website, the server that hands you the app sees the ordinary request for the app itself: the visit. And the page sends privacy-respecting usage analytics to e.syedsarib.com: which pages were visited and where clicks landed, plus anonymous totals such as how many images were compressed, how many megabytes were saved, which formats were involved and how long it took. That is how we learn whether Pocketsize is useful. It never sends your pictures, their names, or what is in them: session recordings hide every word on screen and block the parts that show your files outright. The security policy above forbids every other address.

The code is open

Everything this page claims can be checked a fourth way: by reading the code. Pocketsize is open source (source and licence on GitHub), and the same engine ships as a command line for folders and scripts: pip install pocketsize.
